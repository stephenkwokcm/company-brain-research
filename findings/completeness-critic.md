# completeness-critic — what is still missing, contradictory, or unverified

**Key:** `completeness-critic` · **Date:** 2026-09-10 · Inputs: all 14 reports in `findings/`, the transcript, plus two primary sources I re-fetched myself (GBrain README; Google Research MaleCNS blog).
**Note on method:** this session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was exhausted by the 14 workers, so my own verification was limited to `curl`/WebFetch. Two checks succeeded and are reported below; the rest of this is a gap analysis over what the workers produced.

## TL;DR

- **Question (a) "is it RAG?" is answered well but the answer is internally split and nobody reconciled it.** Four reports cite GBrain's "+31.4 P@5 over vector-only RAG" as proof that a brain beats RAG; two cite the same README's line that the hybrid layer is "roughly neutral" on the one *public* benchmark. **I verified both sentences are in the same file** (`README.md`, lines 12 and 407, fetched 2026-09-10). Different corpora, different metrics. Until that is stated as one sentence, the deliverable will look either credulous or unfair depending on which half the reader sees.
- **Question (b) "combine with the fruit fly work?" was never actually answered.** Fourteen reports produced fragments — FlyHash write-gate, fly Bloom filter, MB readout-gated promotion, `connectome_interpreter` multi-hop, the VFB MCP server, FlyPrompt routing — and **zero produced a single buildable spec with an insertion point, a benchmark, an expected effect size and a kill criterion.** That is the deliverable the user asked for and it does not exist yet.
- **The fruit-fly referent is still ambiguous, and one of the two facts the identification rests on is now in doubt.** Google Research's own blog (fetched today) dates the male-CNS *Cell* paper to **"August 2026"** while both fly reports say **3 September 2026**. The "published seven days before the question" argument — the entire basis for ranking MaleCNS #1 — may rest on the *announcement* date, not the publication date.
- **The biggest missing dimension is the company half of "company brain": permissions.** No report covers ACL/permission-aware retrieval, multi-tenancy, retention schedules, or GDPR erasure — even though three sources the workers found (Vectorize, useaitechdad, colrows) all name governance as *the* unsolved problem, and even though GDPR erasure directly contradicts GBrain's append-only-timeline design. Related: the incumbent comparison set (Glean, Onyx, Dust, Coda Brain, Microsoft Graph) appears only once, in an HN quote.
- **Two whole prior-art literatures that the recommendations depend on are missing:** bi-temporal databases (Snodgrass/TSQL2, SQL:2011 system-versioned tables, Datomic, XTDB) — recommended as intervention #1 by three reports with zero citation to 40 years of DB work — and learned sparse retrieval (SPLADE, uniCOIL, BM42), which is the production answer to "sparse codes for retrieval" and makes the FlyHash-as-index pitch much weaker than the fly reports imply.

## A. Ranked gaps, each with one assignable follow-up task

**1. No buildable "company brain × fruit fly" design.** This is the user's literal question (b) and the corpus answers it only in pieces. *Task:* one agent writes a single design doc: FlyHash + fly Bloom filter as a write-side novelty/dedup gate in front of `put_page`, wired behind GBrain's `docs/designs/VECTOR_BACKENDS.md` pluggable `vector.backend` key (`gbrain-architecture.md` §"fruit-fly hooks"); MB readout-gated promotion (Huang 2024) replacing the scheduled `consolidate` phase; typed signed edges netted at read time (Felsenberg extinction model) as the contradiction alternative to an LLM judge. For each: the exact insertion point in the repo, the benchmark (LongMemEval-S strict `recall_all@5`, BrainBench P@5, and a dedup task à la H3D), the effect size that would count as a win, and the arithmetic that would kill it (see gap 5).

**2. Governance, permissions and multi-tenancy — the "company" half — is uncovered.** Every external critic the workers found lands here (`garry-tan-ecosystem.md` §6: Vectorize "single-operator, unsuitable for multi-tenant"; useaitechdad "multi-tenancy, access control, write conflict resolution… Tan hasn't solved any of those"; colrows "metric consistency"). *Task:* survey permission-aware retrieval (document-level ACLs, late-binding permission filters, per-tenant index isolation, Postgres RLS as GBrain uses it), the enterprise incumbents that already solve it (Glean, Onyx/Danswer, Dust, Coda Brain, Microsoft Graph connectors, Atlassian Rovo), and the compliance collisions — specifically **GDPR erasure vs GBrain's forget-as-fence-rewrite** (`memory-hygiene.md` §3: forget writes a strikethrough + `valid_until` so the deletion is *reconstructible*, which is the opposite of erasure), plus legal hold and retention schedules.

**3. Nobody costed anything.** `garry-tan-ecosystem.md` quotes "under $100 a month sustained for a 25-person company" from `docs/tutorials/company-brain.md` and no one tested it. *Task:* build a bottom-up cost model at 155K pages: embeddings + Voyage rerank-2.5 per query, the 25-phase nightly dream cycle, the contradiction probe (GBrain's own figure: ~$0.0006/judge call, ~$0.50/100 queries, sampled *because* exhaustive pairwise judging is unaffordable), the `takes` extraction receipt ($361.49 for 100,720 takes from 28,256 pages, 2026-05-10), and the human librarian's hours. Output a $/seat/month curve vs corpus size.

**4. The crux experiment is named by four reports and specified by none.** `rag-taxonomy.md` Q3, `skeptic-claims.md` Q5, `memory-systems-landscape.md` Q2 and `neuro-inspired-rag.md` Q2 all say the same thing: nobody has shown that curation/provenance/arbitration improves *downstream answer accuracy* over a well-tuned BM25+vector baseline. *Task:* write the runnable protocol — LongMemEval-S (public) + MemConflict (arXiv:2605.20926) run across the file-and-git family (GBrain, Letta MemFS, Basic Memory) against a tuned hybrid baseline, with the retrieval/answer split reported separately (both MemConflict and gbrain-evals independently find these diverge). Also transfer the one evidence base where curation effect sizes *are* measured: pretraining data-curation ablations (DCLM, FineWeb-Edu).

**5. The index-layer arithmetic that would settle the FlyHash question was never done.** At 155K pages → ~10⁶ chunks × 1536 dims, a pgvector HNSW index is a few GB — likely not the bottleneck. If so, FlyHash-as-index is dead on arithmetic and only the *write-side* fly Bloom filter survives, which sharpens the answer considerably. *Task:* compute index size, build time, and p50/p99 query latency at 10⁵/10⁶/10⁷ chunks for pgvector HNSW vs binary quantization + rescoring vs FlyHash, and add the missing comparator the fly reports never mention: **learned sparse retrieval (SPLADE, uniCOIL, BM42)**, which occupies the same "sparse codes" slot with production-grade numbers.

**6. Bi-temporality is recommended as intervention #1 with no database prior art cited.** `neuro-inspired-rag.md` §"ranked" puts bi-temporal invalidation first; `memory-hygiene.md` §3 and `rag-taxonomy.md` L55 agree. None cites Snodgrass/TSQL2, SQL:2011 system-versioned tables, Datomic, or XTDB — mature, boring, 40-year-old answers to exactly "valid time vs transaction time." A database engineer will notice immediately. *Task:* one paragraph of DB prior art, plus whether Zep/Graphiti/TOKI add anything over SQL:2011 temporal tables other than an LLM extractor.

**7. The librarian is defined three incompatible ways across the corpus** (see contradiction C2) and library science itself is absent. "Librarian" is the load-bearing half of Tan's own definition, and the profession has a century of theory on exactly this — archival appraisal (Schellenberg), *respect des fonds* as provenance, deaccessioning policy, retention schedules. *Task:* adjudicate what GBrain actually automates vs delegates by reading `src/core/cycle.ts` phases against the talk's definition, and bring in one page of records-management theory as the missing frame.

**8. Enterprise/incumbent comparison set is missing.** `memory-systems-landscape.md` surveys 86 OSS agent-memory systems and zero enterprise search products; the only Glean mention in the whole corpus is inside an HN comment quoted by `skeptic-claims.md`. A RAG engineer's first objection is that "company brain" is enterprise knowledge management with a new name. *Task:* a short comparison of Glean / Onyx / Dust / Vectara / Coda Brain / Notion AI against Tan's R1–R7 requirement list from `rag-taxonomy.md`, focused on what they do that GBrain does not (connectors, ACLs, audit) and vice versa (provenance per fact, contradiction probe, skills).

**9. Star counts and ecosystem organicity were never sanity-checked.** OpenClaw at 389,291★ and Hermes at 243,757★ would be roughly the two most-starred repos in GitHub history, both created within ~10 months; claude-mem has 93,572★ with zero lifecycle features (`memory-systems-landscape.md`). `projects-built-on-ideas.md` Q6/Q7 flags the five `b01-gbrain-*` repos appearing the same day with near-identical star counts and an unverified "5,000 stars in 24 hours" claim. *Task:* pull stargazer timelines for gbrain, gstack, openclaw, claude-mem and the b01-* set; flag step functions.

**10. The YC RFS text survives only in a third-party mirror.** Three reports quote the Summer 2026 "Company Brain" RFS from `modelence.com`; the live page rotated to Fall 2026 and GBrain's README deep-links a dead anchor. *Task:* pull the Wayback snapshot of `ycombinator.com/rfs` from ~July 2026 and quote it verbatim, or downgrade every RFS claim to medium confidence.

**11. The two best non-founder case studies rest on one blog post.** Ramp's "Glass" (350+ shared skills, a "Dojo" marketplace) and DoorDash's "Team OS" appear only via `alexlockey.com` (`garry-tan-ecosystem.md`). If real they are stronger evidence than anything in Tan's own corpus. *Task:* chase primary sources.

**12. Skill-pool scaling was never tested at GBrain's own pool size.** `neuro-inspired-rag.md` §8: skill retrieval precision collapses 29.6%@pool-5 → 3.3%@pool-100; GBrain ships 73–142 skills. `context-engineering-skills.md` §4 has the resolver A/B that supposedly rescues this — but on 20 training + 5 held-out fixtures with the held-out set **saturated at 100% for both arms**, so the headline gain is visible only on the training corpus. *Task:* judge whether the resolver claim survives, and whether anyone has measured routing at pool sizes ≥1,000.

## E. Which fruit-fly referent is still ambiguous

Both fly reports converge on the male CNS connectome (~60% confidence) and both reach it via "published one week before the question." That inference is now shakier (C4), and it also has a logical hole: **the connectome is the candidate with the *weakest* answer to the user's actual question.** A wiring diagram cannot be combined with a document brain except by analogy; FlyHash/Spi-Fly/FlyPrompt can. So there are two live readings — "I saw the fly-brain-plays-DOOM news, does that relate?" (connectome, answer: only as a curation parable) and "is there a fly algorithm I could put in my retrieval stack?" (FlyHash lineage, answer: yes, on the write side). The deliverable should present **both**, say plainly that Tan's corpus contains zero biology (two independent greps confirm this), and put one disambiguating question to the user: *"Do you mean the 166,700-neuron male connectome released in early September, or fly-inspired algorithms like FlyHash?"* No worker proposed asking.
