# flyfly-recent-identification — What "the recently published fruit fly brain work" most likely refers to (as of 2026-09-10)

Research worker report. Date of research: 2026-09-10. All claims carry inline source URLs.

## TL;DR

1. **Most likely referent (~60%): the complete male *Drosophila* central-nervous-system connectome**, "Sexual dimorphism in the complete *Drosophila* male central nervous system connectome," *Cell*, **published 3 September 2026** (online 15 Aug 2026), DOI [10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015) — HHMI Janelia + Google Research + MRC LMB/Cambridge. 166,700 neurons, ~125M synapses, 11,710 cell types. It was published **seven days before the question was asked**, and Google Research blogged it.
2. **The reason a *tech* person would be talking about it right now is not the paper — it's the memes.** In the first week of September 2026 developers wired the connectome into game engines: it "played" DOOM, Super Mario 64, Minecraft, Beat Saber, and ran the *Bad Apple* video, within days of the announcement ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/google-mapped-a-fruit-flys-brain-now-its-playing-doom-and-super-mario-64-2000808616), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/programming/google-maps-entire-brain-and-central-nervous-system-of-adult-male-fruit-fly-software-engineers-immediately-make-it-run-doom-ai-powered-3d-model-of-over-166-000-neurons-can-also-play-super-mario-64), [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/0d9bbe70-b2a3-4577-b757-eb8e2b115488)).
3. **Second candidate (~20%): BANC**, "Distributed control circuits across a brain-and-cord connectome," *Nature*, **14 June 2026**, DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10735-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10735-w) — first synapse-resolution map of brain **and** nerve cord in one animal (female). Its headline finding — control is **local and distributed**, not centrally hubbed — is the single most transferable idea to "company brain" architecture.
4. **Third (~10%): the Eon Systems whole-brain fly emulation** ("cyber Drosophila," March 2026; 125k neurons / 50M synapses in MuJoCo, walking and grooming with no RL), built on Shiu et al., *Nature* 634:210–219 (2024). Elon Musk amplified it, which is why it lodged in tech memory.
5. **Fourth (~10%), and the only one that is actually a retrieval algorithm: the fly-olfaction-inspired sparse-hashing lineage** — most recently **Spi-Fly**, "Few-shot, continual learning for spiking neuromorphic olfaction," *Neuromorphic Computing & Engineering*, **24 Aug 2026**, DOI [10.1088/2634-4386/ae9177](https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4386/ae9177), descended from FlyHash (Dasgupta et al., *Science* 2017). **If the user's instinct is "can this be combined with a company brain," this branch is the one with a real mechanism to combine, and the connectome branch is the one they probably *heard about*.**

## Candidate 1 — Male CNS connectome (*Cell*, 3 Sept 2026) — RANK 1

- **Title:** "Sexual dimorphism in the complete *Drosophila* male central nervous system connectome"
- **Authors:** ~90+ co-authors; named leads include Greg Jefferis (MRC LMB), Gerry Rubin (Janelia), Michał Januszewski and Viren Jain (Google Research), with S. Berg, I.R. Beckett, M. Costa, P. Schlegel ([MRC LMB](https://mrclmb.ac.uk/news-events/articles/first-complete-connectome-of-male-fly-central-nervous-system-allows-for-unprecedented-male-female-brain-comparison/))
- **Venue/date:** *Cell*, 3 Sept 2026; online 15 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015. Preprint: [bioRxiv 2025.10.09.680999](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.09.680999v2). Dataset **MaleCNS v1.0 released 8 June 2026**, CC-BY ([Janelia](https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome))
- **Numbers:** 166,700 neurons; ~125 million synaptic connections; 11,710 identifiable cell types; ~5% of male neurons sex-specific or dimorphic (vs <3% in females), but those drive wiring differences in ~12% of male neurons; reconstruction cost ≈44 human-years of labor
- **Access:** [male-cns.janelia.org](https://male-cns.janelia.org/), [neuPrint](https://neuprint.janelia.org/?dataset=male-cns%3Av1.0&qt=findneurons) (queryable via `neuprint-python` / `malecns` R package), [Cell Type Explorer](https://reiserlab.github.io/celltype-explorer-drosophila-male-cns/), [Clio](https://clio.janelia.org), Neuroglancer, bulk downloads
- **Companion papers same week:** Tastekin et al., "The complete gustatory connectome of adult *Drosophila*" (*Cell*); Hoeller et al., "The organization of visual pathways in the *Drosophila* brain" (*Cell*); Rubin et al., "Networks of sexually dimorphic neurons that regulate social behaviors" (*Current Biology*)

**Summary.** First complete CNS wiring diagram of a male fly, with brain and ventral nerve cord joined through an intact neck — so signal paths can be traced end to end, eye to leg. Because a female whole-brain connectome already existed (FlyWire, *Nature*, Oct 2024), this is also the first male-vs-female comparison at synapse resolution: shared sensorimotor infrastructure with a small number of dimorphic "switch" neurons that route the same sensory signal to different behavioral outputs depending on context.

**Why someone connects it to "company brain."** Three hooks. (a) It is a **fully queryable graph over a fixed corpus** — exactly what GBrain claims to be for documents, and neuPrint is literally a graph database you query. (b) The dimorphic-**switch** result is a resolver-table story: same input, different downstream routing, decided by a small number of high-fan-out nodes. That maps onto Tan's "resolver table = org chart." (c) The DOOM/Mario demos made "wiring diagram + trivial dynamics ⇒ behavior" a tech-Twitter talking point in the exact week the question was asked, which is a temptingly direct analogy for "markdown files + trivial retrieval ⇒ company."

**Important caveat to carry forward.** The demos are *not* evidence that structure alone yields function. The connectome gives shape and connectivity, not neuron biophysics or signal propagation; the DOOM mapping was hand-configured by the developer and does not reproduce the fly's real motor pathways (BigGo, above). Alex Warmuth (DOOM) and Jessica Puckett (Mario 64) published source on GitHub; Evan Smith (Georgia Tech) built the Minecraft mod, driving R1–R6 photoreceptors to DNg13 descending neurons with a leaky-integrate-and-fire model ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3971642393686535)).

## Candidate 2 — BANC brain-and-nerve-cord connectome (*Nature*, 14 June 2026) — RANK 2

- **Title:** "Distributed control circuits across a brain-and-cord connectome," Alexander S. Bates et al.; annotation/analysis led by Alex Bates and Helen Yang in Rachel Wilson's lab (HMS), with Wei-Chung Allen Lee (HMS), Mala Murthy and Sebastian Seung (Princeton), and the Drugowitsch lab
- **Venue/date:** *Nature*, 14 June 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10735-w
- **Animal:** one adult **female** *D. melanogaster*, GridTape TEM ([BANC wiki](https://github.com/jasper-tms/the-BANC-fly-connectome/wiki/))
- **Counts:** reported inconsistently across sources — phys.org says ~160,000 neurons; other coverage says ~188,000 neurons and ~199 million predicted synapses. **Treat as medium confidence and check the paper.**
- **Access:** [codex.flywire.ai/banc](https://codex.flywire.ai/banc), [ng.banc.community/view](https://ng.banc.community/view), Harvard Dataverse [10.7910/DVN/8TFGGB](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/8TFGGB)

**Summary.** Effector neurons are driven mostly by sensory neurons **in the same body part**, forming tight local feedback loops; those loops are stitched together by long-range ascending/descending neurons organized into behavior-centric modules. The Drugowitsch lab's "influence score" quantifies propagation and finds hierarchical control assembled *out of* local loops rather than imposed from a central hub ([Drugowitsch lab](https://www.drugowitschlab.org/news/202606-banc_paper/), [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-publish-connectome-fruit-fly-brain.html)).

**Why someone connects it to "company brain."** This is the strongest *conceptual* match in the whole list. Tan's architecture is skill files at the edge plus a resolver table; BANC says the fly does the same thing — competence lives in local circuits, and the "brain" is mostly routing and modulation between them. If you want an empirical brief for "don't build a central orchestrator, build good local skills and a thin resolver," this is it.

## Candidate 3 — Eon Systems whole-brain emulation / "cyber Drosophila" (March 2026) — RANK 3

Eon Systems (chief scientist Philip Shiu) released video of a 125,000-neuron / 50M-synapse fly brain model driving a MuJoCo fly body — walking, grooming, foraging — with no scripting and no RL, ~91% behavioral accuracy; Musk replied "Wow" ([36Kr, 9 Mar 2026](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3715482029142145); [StartupHub, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-research/2026/scientists-recreate-fruit-fly-brain-play-doom)). Built on Shiu et al., "A *Drosophila* computational brain model reveals sensorimotor processing," *Nature* 634(8032):210–219, 2024 ([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9)). Code: [github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain). Michael Andregg's framing — "how much information is captured by the architecture itself, rather than the neuron model" — is the quotable line, and is the strongest available argument for "the schema is the product, the model is rented," which is almost word-for-word Tan's "model quality is rented; your brain you own."

**Rank 3 because** it is six months old and was a *demo release*, not a publication. "Recently published" fits it worse.

## Candidate 4 — Fly-inspired retrieval/memory algorithms — RANK 4 (but highest technical relevance)

- **Spi-Fly** — "Few-shot, continual learning for spiking neuromorphic olfaction," Kevin Max and Yang Shen (OIST) with TU Eindhoven and Kiel, *Neuromorphic Computing & Engineering*, 24 Aug 2026, DOI 10.1088/2634-4386/ae9177 ([TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-08-fruit-fly-algorithm-odors-samples.html)). Three layers: random sparse projection into 1,000 hidden neurons producing a sparse "barcode," then an associative rule at the output. Few-shot learning from a handful of samples and **continual learning with no catastrophic forgetting** — though it still trails conventional ML on raw accuracy, and mixed-background performance is unvalidated.
- **FlyHash lineage** — the fly olfactory circuit is a locality-sensitive hash that expands into high dimension and sparsifies via winner-take-all (~2,000 dims, ~95% sparse), unlike classical LSH which compresses. Follow-ups: BioHash / "Bio-Inspired Hashing for Unsupervised Similarity Search" ([arXiv:2001.04907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04907)), SoftHash, and "Approximate Vector Set Search Inspired by Fly Olfactory Neural System" ([arXiv:2412.03301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03301)). The mushroom-body novelty circuit (MBON responses decay with familiarity) is a **dedup / staleness primitive**, which is precisely the hygiene problem Tan names.
- **Fly-connectomic Graph Model** — "Whole-Brain Connectomic Graph Model Enables Whole-Body Locomotion Control in Fruit Fly," Zehao Jin, Yaoye Zhu, Chen Zhang, Yanan Sui, [arXiv:2602.17997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997) (v1 20 Feb 2026, v3 14 Jun 2026): uses the connectome graph itself as an RL policy architecture, reporting better sample efficiency than baselines.
- **Connectome-Based Modelling Reveals Orientation Maps in the *Drosophila* Optic Lobe**, [arXiv:2609.01330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01330) (Sept 2026) — orientation maps emerge from connectome-constrained spiking dynamics alone.
- **ConnectomeBench: Can LLMs Proofread the Connectome?** Jeff Brown, Andrew Kirjner, Annika Vivekananthan, Ed Boyden, [arXiv:2511.05542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05542), NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks. Runs the arrow *backwards*: LLMs as connectome librarians. 52–82% balanced accuracy on segment identification (chance 20–25%), 75–85% on split errors (chance 50%), poor on merge errors. Directly relevant to Tan's "librarian" role — LLMs are decent at *identifying* and *splitting* but bad at *merging*, which is exactly the contradiction-arbitration failure mode.

## Relevance to company-brain / RAG / fruit-fly question

The user has, probably unknowingly, conflated two different fly-brain stories, and the honest answer separates them:

**The connectome story (what they almost certainly read about)** is a *cartography* result. It says: here is a complete, curated, provenance-tracked, versioned, queryable graph over a bounded corpus, built at a cost of ~44 human-years, with every node typed and cross-referenced against a second dataset. That is not a RAG analogy — it is a **curation analogy**, and it is the strongest possible illustration of Tan's actual thesis. The fly connectome's value is not that you can search it; it is that 44 years of human annotation made it *worth searching*. "Retrieval is easy. Being worth retrieving from is the product." The MaleCNS release is a live demonstration that a well-curated 166,700-node graph gets useful third-party applications within *days* of release, while the raw EM volume sat unusable for years.

**The algorithmic story (what would actually combine)** is FlyHash/Spi-Fly. Concretely combinable pieces: (a) sparse high-dimensional expansion as a complement to dense embeddings for the "cold reference" tier, where sparse codes are cheap to store and interpretable per-dimension; (b) mushroom-body novelty detection as an ingest filter — a cheap "have I already got this?" gate before writing a new page, which is the anti-garbage-dump mechanism; (c) Spi-Fly's continual learning without catastrophic forgetting as a model for hot-memory updates that don't clobber cold reference.

**BANC is the architectural brief.** Distributed local sensorimotor loops linked by ascending/descending modules is, structurally, "skill files at the edge + a thin resolver," and it is the empirical counter-argument to building one giant central agent.

**Caution to flag to the orchestrator:** none of this is a citation the *talk* makes. I found **no evidence Garry Tan or GBrain has ever mentioned fruit flies or connectomics** — searching "Garry Tan fruit fly brain GBrain" returns only GBrain material (repo: [github.com/garrytan/gbrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain), released 5 April 2026). The fruit-fly connection is the user's own synthesis. Also: I found **no paper anywhere combining connectome-inspired architecture with RAG or knowledge-graph retrieval** — that search came back empty. If the user wants to build that, it appears to be genuinely unclaimed ground, which is worth telling them.
