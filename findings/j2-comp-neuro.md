## 0. The panel, judged from this seat, in one paragraph

This is a better-behaved set of transplants than the literature it draws on. Nobody proposes connectome-derived wiring (all five either ignore it or cite `arXiv:2604.04033`'s degree-preserving null against it); nobody buries doomfly's failed validation gates; nobody quotes "16.0% → 44.8% mAP" without the equal-hash-length qualifier once `d4` supplies it; three of the five pre-register a kill criterion that would delete the biology from their own design, and one of them (`d4`) already ran it and published the failure. That is unusual and it should be said before the deductions. The deductions are real, though, and they cluster in one place: **the two-compartment and two-timescale claims.** `d1` and `d3` both build on the fly Bloom filter, and both attach it to compartment anatomy it does not belong to — `d1` by asserting that γ and α compartments read the same Kenyon-cell population (they do not), `d3` by writing down an update rule that is not PNAS 2018 Eq. 2 (§3.C below). `d2` is the cleanest biological citation set in the panel and is also the only design that ships a **zero-fly control arm** (B5/K4), which from this seat is the single most valuable methodological device anyone in the panel proposed. `d5` makes no biological claim at all and says so four separate times, which I cannot penalise but also cannot credit as an answer to question (b).

**Ranking:** `d2` (41) > `d4` (40) > `d1` (39) > `d3` (36) > `d5` (35). The winner to graft into is **`d2`**; `d4` ranks second as a *document*, not as a build (its own verdict is DO NOT BUILD, and it is right).

## 2. Per-design rationale

### d2 — lifecycle (41, BUILD)

**Rationale.** This is the only design whose problem statement is a *measured defect rather than a hypothesis*: gbrain#3042 shows one `dream --phase consolidate` run processing **62 buckets and promoting 0 facts into 0 takes**, because every ≥3-fact bucket tops out at cosine **0.844** under a 0.85 threshold, while the human-gated queue in #3269 reached **32,500 rows growing 2–6k/day**. Promotion fires on duplication and forgetting is a read-time sort key that never writes back — that is backwards, and Huang et al. (*Nature* 634:1141, 2024) is a legitimate source for the inversion. The biology is cited accurately throughout (dDA1 vs DAMB as two channels, Berry 2015's sleep-blocks-forgetting, Felsenberg's coexisting compartmental traces), the currency mismatch is stated in the same breath every time, and the report pre-registers a **pooled null** as its modal outcome. Its two gates are the best in the panel: **K5** measures the load-bearing untested assumption g07 named — the AUROC of `last_retrieved_at`/`retrieval_bouts` for predicting "cited in a correct answer," `< 0.60` kills L1 — for **one day and $0**, before any code; and **K4** runs a TinyLFU + CREW + SQL:2011 arm with **zero fly content** and commits to writing "the fruit-fly framing contributed nothing measurable" in those words if it ties within 1.0 pp.

**Deductions.** Feasibility 8 not 9 because L2 is the piece most likely to be refused upstream under GBrain's own D17 doctrine, and because `promotion_gate=readout` on a brain with `search.track_retrieval` disabled promotes *nothing* — worse than today (d2 catches this, risk #10). Novelty 7 because d2 concedes item-by-item that W-TinyLFU, CREW's acquisition-rate law and SQL:2011 cover most of the ground; the fly's residue is three structural properties, not an algorithm. Evidence 9 not 10 for two biology items in §3 below (the τ_refute direction and the 100× bout rescaling), neither of which is hidden.

**Verdict: BUILD**, in this order — L0 (readout ledger, half a day, cache-safe, verified against the `bump_page_generation_fn()` allow-list) and the `HALFLIFE_DAYS` fix (a verified 1.44× error: `exp(-age/7)` is 36.8% at 7 days, so the true half-life is 4.85 d) ship immediately; L1 only after K5 clears 0.60; L2 dry-run only; L3 only after `g01`'s `WITHOUT OVERLAPS` constraint and `d5`'s B1 lineage exist, because a ledger keyed on a mutable slug is not an audit trail.

### d4 — index adversary (40, KILL the fly backend / BUILD D4-ALT)

**Rationale.** The only design in the panel that ran its own experiment, and it ran the one four prior reports flagged as never run by anyone: FlyHash and BioHash **on real 1,024-d text embeddings**, scored against `binary_quantize()` in pgvector 0.8.6 on the same corpus. All three pre-registered criteria fired — at equal bytes, sign binarization beats FlyHash **0.7577 vs 0.4948** raw and **0.9918 vs 0.8339** after rescoring; the FlyHash column measures **2.4× the bytes and 2.2× the latency** of `bit(1024)`; and the only configuration reaching parity is refused verbatim by the server (`sparsevec cannot have more than 1000 non-zero elements for hnsw index`). What earns the 10 on evidence is not the kill, it is the **exoneration attached to it**: at equal *hash length* — the *Science* 2017 protocol — FlyHash beats random-hyperplane LSH by **+82% relative** (0.4948 vs 0.2712), so the fly result replicates on modern text embeddings and the corpus should stop implying otherwise. That is what a reviewer wants from an adversary: separate the scientific claim from the engineering claim and adjudicate them independently.

**Deductions.** Benefit 6 because the report says so itself — the ANN scan is **0.14% of a ~3,272 ms answered question** (`g02`), so "there is no version of this proposal whose success would be measurable by a user"; D4-ALT's payoff is a hosting-tier one ($410/mo 2XL → $110/mo Large at 1M pages, 13,726 → 609 B/row). Novelty 6 because the design it actually recommends is a documented pgvector recipe with upstream tests — the novelty lives in the measurements, which are already paid for under evidence.

**Verdict: KILL the fly backend** — and I endorse d4's instruction that the judging phase treat any proposal listing FlyHash or BioHash under `vector.backend` as refuted rather than open. **BUILD D4-ALT** (3–5 dev-days, one generated column) on its storage case alone, and carry d4's two survivals forward: the corrected dedup baseline (free ANN probe **0.843**, `binary_quantize` **0.834**, lexical SimHash **0.590** and **0.263** on paraphrase-shaped edits), and the porting fact that per-stimulus centring is a WTA no-op on L2-normalised embeddings.

### d1 — write gate (39, PILOT)

**Rationale.** The best *derivation* in the panel. g07's single biggest caveat on the one Deep-fidelity mechanism was "δ and ε are free parameters with no data-derived values," and d1 closes it: from PNAS Eq. 2 it derives `1 − δ = m/(k·n)` and `ε = w*/n`, the floor `n ≥ m/k`, and the duplicate-signal identity **one duplicate insert ≡ m/(k·r) days of drift**. I checked all four; they follow from the paper's own update rule and the conclusion they force — that one `(δ, ε)` pair cannot deliver both a duplicate detector and a staleness clock, because the duplicate signal is pinned to `H_min/H` of the dynamic range — is correct and is not in the paper. The scoping is disciplined: §1 lists four refuted proposals and refuses to re-litigate them, §11 concedes that `content_hash` owns exact dedup, SimHash owns lexical near-duplicates, `1 − max cosine` owns the distance axis and SQL owns the long horizon, leaving exactly one unoccupied cell — **content-level recurrence after dormancy**, which GBrain's contradiction probe structurally cannot reach because its own pipeline discards pairs more than 30 days apart. K2 is the right crux and it costs **~200 lines and four days with no GBrain code touched**.

**Deductions.** Evidence 8, not higher, for the compartment error in §2.3 (§3.A below) and the α′3/α3 splice (§3.B). Benefit 6 because d1's own economics are small — **$7–19/month saved** against a ~$96/month curation line, break-even at 0.7% of writes suppressed — and because the 15–20% LLM-call reduction it predicts is borrowed from SAGE, i.e. it is the *slot's* benefit, not the fly's. Its own stated confidence that the fly beats the free baselines is 20% overall.

**Verdict: PILOT.** Run K2 as a standalone offline experiment in week 1. Two amendments before it runs: score against `d4`'s baselines (free ANN probe 0.843, `binary_quantize` 0.834), **not** against lexical SimHash, which `d4` showed collapses to 0.263 on exactly the paraphrase case a company brain faces; and add the two biology ablations in §4 (adaptive-k, fan-in sweep). If K2 fails, ship the seam with `scorer: max-cosine` and close the branch — which is d1's own instruction and it should be honoured.

### d3 — router (36, BUILD stage 1 / KILL the fly gate)

**Rationale.** The most valuable *destructive* work in the panel on question (b), and I credit it heavily: it retires checkpoint decision (iii) with its own measurement, having first corrected the attribution — FlyPrompt's REAR is a **dense Gaussian matrix + ReLU with no winner-take-all, no sparse binary projection and no enforced activity level**, i.e. RanPAC (NeurIPS 2023) with a fly sentence in the abstract. It then ran the honest trigger-blind condition on GBrain's own 73 skills and 271 fixtures and found REAR at **−1.1 pp** and FlyHash k-WTA at **−1.4 to −5.9 pp** against a no-training nearest centroid, with the ridge λ oracle-selected in the expansion arms' favour. That is the third socket the fly hash has lost. Its non-fly findings are first-rate: **303/303 → 0/271** under leave-one-trigger-out, with 24 intents silently misrouting; the STRICT-vs-LENIENT recomputation showing compression costs **18–27 pp** where the README reports a gain; and a context bill of **$528–$1,077/month** at 150–306 skills against `g02`'s **$225/month** for the entire brain.

**Deductions.** Evidence 7, down from 9 on the measurements, for a specific and consequential error: **the §4 sketch is not PNAS 2018 Eq. 2** (§3.C). Novelty 6 because the surviving design is RAG-MCP (arXiv:2505.03275) applied to skills, and the report says so; and because B10 may make the whole component redundant at the platform layer. Benefit 7 — the token saving is the largest measured dollar effect in the panel, but it is not a fly result.

**Verdict: BUILD** the non-biological stage 1 plus RouteBench-150 and the LOTO regression test; **KILL** the fly abstention gate as specified. It can be reopened only with the corrected update rule and PNAS's own sparsity regime — and at that point it is d1's mechanism in a second socket, so run d1's K2 first and let one experiment answer both.

### d5 — curation process (35, PILOT)

**Rationale.** Excellent archival engineering and the most honest self-assessment in the panel: it rates itself **zero** as fly biology (not superficial — zero), quotes g07's warning against blurring connectome data practice with fly neural algorithms, flags its own motivated-reasoning risk given that it is the last branch of question (b) standing, and pre-registers K4 to report a null. Three items are strong and cheap: **B9** — ConnectomeBench (arXiv:2511.05542) measures LLMs at 52–82% balanced accuracy on segment identification and 75–85% on split errors but "generally struggling on merge error identification," while GBrain's one automatic destructive operation is a slug-normalising **merge** capped at 50 pages/run; inverting that is one day and it is the only recommendation in the entire panel backed by a benchmark that measured exactly the decision at issue. **B5** (confidence carried with its scale and producer, verified beats predicted) and **B3** (two-tier status vocabulary with a build-breaking exhaustiveness assertion) are each ≤2 days. CAVE's published operating numbers (1.8B annotations, >4M edits from >500 users, as-of at **525 → 978 → 1,385 ms**, ~$500/month) are the kind of cost disclosure this corpus otherwise lacks entirely.

**Deductions.** Feasibility 6 — B1 re-keys `facts.entity_slug` and rebuilds `page_versions` as an immutable substrate on a live 155,795-page brain; the report's own honest estimate is **6–8 weeks** to production, the largest bill in the panel, and its §8.1 identifies the fatal asymmetry: CAVE's guarantees are all downstream of an immutable EM volume that a company brain simply does not have. Novelty 6 and Benefit 7 because §6 concedes item-by-item that W3C Web Annotation, SQL:2011, OBO Foundry, PROV-O and LakeFS/Dolt do it better, with one exception (the immutable-anchor + lineage + time-resolved-identity triple).

**Verdict: PILOT.** Ship B3, B7, B8 and B9 now (≤2 days each, B9 benchmark-backed). Hold B1/B2 behind K1's anchor-breakage measurement — if real slug/anchor breakage over 30 days is <2%, ship `page_lineage` alone and stop. And the synthesis must not present this as an answer to "can the company brain be combined with fly-brain work": it is an answer to "what did the labs that mapped the fly build around the data," which is a different and, from this seat, a more useful question honestly labelled.

## 3. Biology-fidelity findings (the deductions, in full)

These are the specific items my seat exists to catch. None is fatal; all should be corrected before the synthesis repeats them.

**A. `d1` §2.3 — "reading the same Kenyon-cell population" is false.** The ~2,000 Kenyon cells divide into three classes — γ, α′β′ and αβ — and each class innervates its own lobe. Compartments *along one lobe* (γ1…γ5) share KC axons; **γ and α compartments do not**. MBON-γ1pedc>α/β reads γ KCs; MBON-α3 reads αβ KCs; MBON-α′3 reads α′β′ KCs. So "two MBONs with different time constants reading the same KCs" is not the anatomy Huang et al. describe. d1's actual construction — one FlyHash code with a nested top-128 / top-64 — is an engineering simplification, and arguably a good one (it guarantees `n_α ≤ n_γ`, which is what makes the `impossible` corner a genuine invariant and a free `doctor` check). But the sentence must be re-written, and §2.3's "Analogy" rating is generous once it is: the topology being transplanted is not the topology being cited.

**B. `d1` splices the novelty compartment onto the valence-memory compartments.** MBON-α′3 (Hattori et al., *Cell* 169:956, 2017) is a **familiarity/novelty** readout; the γ→α consolidation cascade in Huang 2024 is about **associative valence**. There is no published evidence that the fly runs *novelty* filters at two time constants, and PNAS 2018's own Discussion item (ii) — which d1 quotes correctly — is an open question, not a result. d1's §10.1 concedes the separability assumption is the thing the paper is least confident about, which is the right disclosure; but the framing "Yes: other MBONs, with other time constants, reading the same KCs — which is what Huang 2024 measured six years later" reads as though Huang answered PNAS's question. Huang did not. The two-timescale novelty filter is d1's invention and should be labelled as such; it is a good invention and it does not need the borrowed authority.

**C. `d3` §4 writes down the wrong update rule for the mechanism it calls "Deep fidelity."** PNAS 2018 Eq. 2 decays the **active** weights multiplicatively (`w(i) ← w(i)·δ` for `i ∈ A(x)`) and increases the **inactive** ones additively (`w(i) ← w(i) + ε` for `i ∉ A(x)`), on an **inverted** filter initialised to 1 so novel items score high. `d3`'s sketch has `w_s ← (1−ε)·w_s` for **all** `i` and `w_s[i] ← w_s[i] + δ` for `i ∈ h` — a familiarity filter with global decay and the roles of δ and ε swapped. The property d3 is buying (staleness for free, distance and time in one O(1)-state number) comes precisely from the **complementarity** of the two updates; a globally-decaying filter does not have it. Second error in the same section: `m = 40d`, `ρ = 5%` → 2,048 winners is the *Science* 2017 **hashing** regime; PNAS 2018 deliberately runs far sparser tags (`k ∈ [5, 50]`, `m = 30n`) to suppress collisions, and the biological α′3 readout samples ~350 KCs, not 2,048. `d1` got both of these right and `d3` did not — which is why the same mechanism should be settled once, in `d1`'s K2, rather than twice.

**D. `d2`'s `τ_refute` (730 d) > `τ_support` (365 d) runs opposite to the fly.** Felsenberg 2018 plus the spontaneous-recovery literature has the extinction (opposing) trace as the **labile** one — the original aversive memory recovers over time, which is why extinction is not erasure. d2 explicitly labels the asymmetry "a policy choice I am making, not biology," which is exactly right practice and is why this costs only a fraction of a point. But the report should add one clause saying the direction is *inverted* relative to the circuit it cites, so nobody downstream reads compartmental decay asymmetry in this direction as a fly result.

**E. `d2`'s bout constants are a ~100× rescaling.** Huang's "roughly three conditioning bouts" are spaced at inter-stimulus intervals on the order of **15 minutes**; d2's refractory is **24 hours** ("a different working day"). The rescaling is disclosed and operationally sensible, but it means "three spaced bouts" carries no biological content beyond the integer 3. Say so, or the `B = 3` default will be defended in review on Huang's authority.

**F. `d4`'s "normalisation is a mathematical no-op" is right for the operation it implemented and over-general for the fly's.** The algebra is correct: with constant fan-in `f`, `M(x − c·1) = Mx − c·f·1`, a uniform shift, so winner-take-all is invariant — and scalar division is likewise invariant. But the fly's ORN→PN transformation (Olsen, Bhandawat & Wilson, *Neuron* 2010) is a **nonlinear divisive gain control** whose divisor is the summed ORN population response; it compresses and *reshapes* the PN pattern, it does not merely scale it. d4's practical conclusion — don't bother implementing it for L2-normalised embeddings — stands. The sentence "one of the three published departures from LSH silently does not transfer" should become "the idealised scalar form of it does not."

**G. What nobody got wrong, and should be recorded as such.** No design proposes connectome-derived wiring; `d3` and `d5` both cite Dhiman (arXiv:2604.04033) against it. `d1` and `g07` both carry doomfly's self-reported failure of its visual, conditioning and survival validation gates. `d2` engages Xie & Ocker (arXiv:2509.19351) — pruning KC→MBON synapses degrades classification much as ablation does, mature KCs worst — and answers it correctly with demote-not-delete plus reset-on-recall, rather than waving it off. `d2` also names the **absent APL analogue** as the reason its promotion loop has no sparsity budget, which is the single most sophisticated piece of fly reasoning anywhere in the panel.

**H. The one negative result nobody built an arm for: sparsity is not fixed in the fly.** KC sparseness (~5–10%) is enforced *dynamically* by APL's GABAergic feedback proportional to total KC drive (Lin, Bygrave, de Calignon, Lee & Miesenböck, *Nat. Neurosci.* 17:559, 2014), and Gkanias et al. list APL among their model's candidly-named omissions. Every design in this panel uses a **fixed top-k**. For a bursty ingest stream — which company-brain ingest is — fixed-k and adaptive-k behave differently, and nobody tests it. See §4, graft 3.

## 4. Best ideas to graft from lower-ranked designs into the winner (`d2`)

Ordered by value per engineering day. The first three are the ones I would not ship `d2` without.

1. **`d5` B5 — confidence with its scale and producer (`confidence_scale`, `confidence_producer`, `verified_value` wins over predicted).** `d2`'s `net(c) = Σ sign·magnitude·exp(−age/τ)` sums magnitudes produced by different extractors, model versions and human judgements as though they were commensurable — which is exactly the error VFB's published policy forbids ("carried through unchanged… a value should be read against the publication it came from"). This is a two-day schema change that makes `d2`'s central function meaningful. **Highest-value graft in the list.**
2. **`d5` B1 — `page_lineage` as a precondition, not a competitor.** `d2`'s L3 ledger and `g01`'s `WITHOUT OVERLAPS` constraint both key on `facts.entity_slug`, a bare mutable `TEXT` with no FK that the nightly cycle itself rewrites (up to 50 pages/run, with nowhere to record that a merge happened). A ledger keyed on a string that moves is not an audit trail. Ship the lineage table (one day) even if B1's anchoring machinery is deferred behind K1.
3. **`d1`'s parameter laws + two biology ablations, folded into `d2`'s undefined interference term `h(i)`.** `d2`'s forgetting daemon has an interference term with no definition; g07 notes the fly Bloom filter computes exactly that for free. Take `d1`'s closed forms (`1 − δ = m/(k·n)`, `ε = w*/n`, floor `n ≥ m/k`) so the term has derived rather than invented constants, and add the two arms my seat says are missing: **(a) adaptive-k** — set the winner count by thresholding total drive rather than fixing top-k, which is what APL actually does (§3.H); **(b) a fan-in sweep** over `s ∈ {6, 12, 96, 154}` — the fly's ~6 claws is an *absolute* number that Litwin-Kumar et al. (*Neuron* 93:1153, 2017) show optimises dimensionality for the fly's input dimension and noise, so neither 6 nor `d1`'s 6.25% ratio transfers to d=1536 without re-deriving. Both are one extra row in K2's table.
4. **`d1`'s K2, run once, answering both sockets.** Recurrence-after-dormancy AUROC at G ∈ {7, 14, 25} days, `fly-bank` vs `fly-single` vs max-cosine vs SimHash vs SAGE, offline, ~200 lines, four days, ~$0. It is the only test of the only property nothing else supplies, and it settles `d3`'s abstention gate at the same time. Pair it with `d2`'s K5 in the same pre-registration.
5. **`d4`'s corrected baselines, made mandatory for every gate arm.** Free ANN probe **0.843**, `binary_quantize` **0.834**, FlyHash k=64 **0.800**, lexical SimHash **0.590** and **0.263** on paraphrase-shaped edits. Any novelty/dedup component benchmarked against lexical SimHash alone is being graded against the weakest available opponent.
6. **`d1`'s `novelty_band='impossible'` doctor check, generalised.** A state-space corner that cannot occur if the parameters and state are correct, wired to a `doctor` warning at >1% — a free corruption detector. `d2` should ship the equivalent invariants on `retrieval_bouts` (bouts > 0 with `last_bout_at` NULL) and `suppression` (suppression > 0 on a fact revived this cycle), given that gbrain#3889 documents a run where every judge call failed, the probe reported "0 contradictions," and `doctor` showed `[OK]`.
7. **`d1`'s route-don't-block doctrine and per-source state scoping.** Never refuse a write, only annotate and route; scope any filter or suppression state per `source_id` so a hostile source cannot depress another's; make every budget a hard cap so an adversary cannot induce unbounded judge spend. `d2`'s suppression daemon is the same attack surface (OWASP ASI06) and inherits the same three mitigations for free.
8. **`d3`'s LOTO discipline, applied to every eval `d2` ships.** Strip the tokens a fixture was built from and re-score: GBrain's routing benchmark went **303/303 → 0/271** under that test, with 24 silent misroutes. And `d3`'s STRICT-vs-LENIENT rule — never report a metric that grants one arm partial credit the others cannot earn. Both are regression tests, not opinions.
9. **`d3`'s write-back pattern.** Route decisions land in `facts` with `kind='event'` and inherit decay, consolidation and audit for free. `d2`'s `lifecycle_events` should follow the same pattern rather than being a bespoke log, so the lifecycle's own telemetry is subject to the lifecycle.
10. **`d5` B9 — human-gate merges, automate splits and identification.** One day, and it is the only panel recommendation backed by a benchmark that measured exactly this decision (ConnectomeBench: 75–85% on split errors, "generally struggling" on merges, against GBrain's automatic 50-merges-per-run).
11. **`d5`'s graded uncertainty vocabulary, replacing `d2`'s bare sign+magnitude.** FlyWire's `is_hemilineage` is not a float — it is `H`, `H?`, `Hp`, `H/L`, `H(NT)`, `H_2NT`, where **the reason for the doubt is encoded in the label**. `d2` already copies the contradiction probe's six verdict classes into `claim_evidence.verdict`; make that class the primary carrier and the float secondary.
12. **`d4`'s D4-ALT as an orthogonal budget release.** 3–5 dev-days, 13,726 → 609 B/row, $410/mo → $110/mo at the million-page tier. It buys nothing in quality and it pays for the curation work that does — which, given `g02`'s finding that write-side curation costs **222×–4,514×** embedding, is the right trade to make explicit.
13. **The `HALFLIFE_DAYS` fix, before anything calibrates against it.** `exp(−age/7)` is 36.8% at 7 days; the true half-life is **4.85 days**; every kind decays **1.44× faster than its name claims**. Rename to `TIME_CONSTANT_DAYS` or multiply by `1/ln 2`, and pin the choice in tests. Any decay parameter in any design tuned against the current table is tuned against a 44% error.

## 5. What I would tell the synthesis, from this seat

Three sentences, and I would not soften any of them.

1. **After this round, exactly one fly mechanism is still standing, and it is standing on one leg.** The fly Bloom filter (PNAS 2018) is the only Deep-fidelity transplant in the inventory, its only unoccupied cell is content-level recurrence after dormancy, its δ and ε now have derived values for the first time (`d1` §3), and it has still never been run on text. Everything else — FlyHash as index (`d4`, measured), as quantizer (`d4`), as dedup hash (`v10b`, and `d4` corrects the incumbent), as routing representation (`d3`, measured), and connectome topology as a wiring prior (arXiv:2604.04033) — is refuted with first-party numbers by this panel's own work.
2. **The panel's best answer to question (b) contains no neuroscience, and it says so.** `d5` is process transfer from the *labs* that mapped the fly, not from the fly, and the honest version of the user's answer says the algorithms mostly do not port while the curation practice does. A reader who wanted fly biology in their retrieval stack should be told the branch was measured and closed, not handed a connectome-branded substitute.
3. **The methodological device worth carrying out of this project is `d2`'s K4** — a zero-biology control arm with a pre-committed sentence to publish if it ties. Bio-inspired systems papers almost never run one. This panel proposed it, and if the synthesis carries one habit forward, it should be that one.
